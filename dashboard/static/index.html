What counts as a violation

Every detector asks one question: is there a basket of contracts that pays at least W dollars in every outcome, and can it be bought for less than W after Kalshi's fees? Every leg is a taker buy at the ask; selling YES at the bid is the same trade as buying NO at 1 − bid.

  • ladder — "X above K₁" and "X above K₂" with K₁ < K₂: buy YES on K₁ and NO on K₂; pays at least $1. A violation means bid(K₂) > ask(K₁).
  • set_short — mutually exclusive outcomes: buy NO on all n; pays at least n − 1.
  • set_long — buy YES on all n; pays exactly $1 only if the set is complete, so it is only evaluated once every other market in the event has closed.
  • yes_no_cross — YES and NO of one market for under $1. The matching engine should make this impossible; seeing one points at stale data.

Fees

Taker fee = roundup(0.07 × m × C × P × (1 − P)), rounded up to the cent once per order; m is the series multiplier read from the API. Edges are walked through the book level by level and the size with the best net P&L is kept.

Tradeable

A violation is shown as tradeable when every leg traded at least contracts in the 24 h before the market list was built, at least baskets sit at the best prices, and every leg's bid–ask spread is at most .

Lifetimes and reaction time

Books are polled about once a second, so a lifetime is known only as an interval: seen from poll ts to te means it lived between te − ts and tnext − tprev. Open episodes count as censored in the Kaplan–Meier estimate. Reaction time = detection lag (uniform over the poll interval) + book fetch + compute + order round trip, measured with python -m mispricing.latency.

Replay

Each alert becomes immediate-or-cancel limit buys on every leg (capped at 500 baskets) that land after a sampled reaction time and fill against the next recorded book. If the legs' books did not change in between, the fill is certain; if they changed, the new book is used. Unhedged contracts are sold back into the bids at a second fee. No order is ever sent.
